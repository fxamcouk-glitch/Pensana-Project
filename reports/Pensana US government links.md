# US records show Pensana's backing is early-stage

US government websites confirm two things about Pensana Plc (LSE: PRE) and its Longonjo rare earths project in Angola, and nothing more. The first is a **US$3.4m DFC technical-assistance grant**, of which **US$1.95m had been paid out** when its performance period ended on 24 September 2026 ([USAspending](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/ASST_NON_9000116961_077)). The second is a **pending EXIM environmental listing**, posted 18 February 2026, with the environmental and social impact assessment (ESIA) "not yet publicly available" ([EXIM pending transactions](https://www.exim.gov/policies/exim-bank-and-environment/pending-transactions)). No US agency has approved a loan, guarantee, equity stake or offtake for Pensana. The Federal Register contains no document naming Pensana, Longonjo or Ozango ([Federal Register API](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json?conditions[term]=Pensana)). The White House fact sheet for the August 2026 mining roundtable that Pensana's CEO attended does not mention the company ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/08/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-announces-billions-in-new-deals-and-investments-to-power-american-mining/)). In US company filings, Pensana appears once: Energy Fuels lists it as one of several upstream MOUs held by VAC, the magnet maker Energy Fuels is acquiring ([Energy Fuels 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1385849/000106299326003630/exhibit99-1.htm)). Pensana has no SEC registration, and no FARA registration exists for Pensana, Ozango or Cascade. So the US relationship is real, but it is at application and feasibility stage. Pensana's wording overstates it, most clearly the March 2026 description of its debt as "U.S. EXIM Guaranteed" ([RNS 4 Mar 2026](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/prn/pensana--pre/increased-us-165-million-strategic-investment-/9457728)). Nothing in this report alleges wrongdoing. The gap is between announced intent and recorded commitment, and the earliest public evidence of progress would be EXIM posting the Longonjo ESIA.

Labels used throughout: **Confirmed** means shown in a primary record (a .gov page or database, an SEC filing, or a registry). **Reported** means stated by Pensana, a counterparty or the press, but not shown in a primary record. **Inferred** is this report's analysis. **Unknown** means it could not be established.

## EXIM has an open Longonjo file, but no approval

**Confirmed.** EXIM's list of pending Environmental Category A transactions includes "Pensana Longonjo Rare Earths Mine". It gives the location as 60 km west of Huambo and 30 km north of Benguela, the reference **AP778661XX**, a posting date of **18 February 2026**, CO2 "TBD" and an ESIA that is "Not yet publicly available" ([EXIM pending transactions](https://www.exim.gov/policies/exim-bank-and-environment/pending-transactions)). This is EXIM's own record that an application for Longonjo exists, so the engagement goes beyond anything Pensana has said. The listing names no borrower, amount, exporter or bank. Category A projects must have an ESIA or equivalent document made public before the board decides, and EXIM's procedures require it to be public for at least 30 days before final approval. Because the ESIA has not been posted after eight months, **final approval cannot come until at least 30 days after it is posted**. Long waits are not unusual: NioCorp's Elk Creek project, also on the list, has been there since November 2023 without an ESIA ([EXIM pending transactions](https://www.exim.gov/policies/exim-bank-and-environment/pending-transactions)).

**Confirmed absence.** EXIM published twelve Federal Register notices of applications for final commitment of over US$100m between November 2025 and 14 October 2026. Every one was read in full. The obligors included Perpetua Resources, Mesabi Metallics and Silk Way West Airlines, and **none relates to Pensana, Angola, rare earths or Absa** ([Federal Register EXIM notices](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json?conditions[agencies][]=export-import-bank&conditions[publication_date][gte]=2025-09-01); [e.g. 14 Oct 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/14/2026-20933/application-for-final-commitment-for-a-long-term-loan-or-financial-guarantee-in-excess-of-100)). EXIM has issued no board agenda item or press release naming Pensana. The only Sunshine Act meeting notice in the period dealt with a working-capital programme ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/22/2026-07848/sunshine-act-meetings)). **Inferred:** a guarantee covering most of a US$160m loan is above the US$100m threshold that, in practice, produces a Federal Register notice and a notification to Congress before final commitment. Their absence shows the Longonjo file had not reached that stage by mid-October 2026.

**Reported.** EXIM's support of up to 100% of the US$160m Absa-led debt rests on a Letter of Interest that Pensana announced on 28 October 2025 ([Pensana RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/prn/pensana--pre/u-s-exim-debt-funding-to-accelerate-u-s-min-/9197987)). EXIM does not publish LOIs. CSIS lists "$160 million to Pensana's Longonjo project in Angola" among EXIM rare-earth LOIs worth **nearly $4 billion in total**, which shows how common such letters are ([CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)). The conditions matter more than the letter. EXIM's Supply Chain Resiliency Initiative (SCRI) funds foreign projects that have "signed long-term 'off-take' contracts with U.S. companies". The amount financed depends on the share of output going to the US, and the project cannot be controlled by the PRC ([EXIM SCRI](https://exim.gov/about/special-initiatives/supply-chain-resiliency-initiative)). Every Longonjo offtake is a non-binding MOU, so **the condition that would set the size of EXIM's financing has not yet been met** (inferred from the SCRI rules and Pensana's own disclosures).

## DFC's grant is the only US money that has moved

**Confirmed.** During President Biden's visit to Angola on 4 December 2024, DFC announced "a $3.4 million technical assistance grant to Pensana to conduct feasibility studies" for a rare earth mine and refinery at Longonjo, alongside the US$553m Lobito Atlantic Railway loan ([DFC press release](https://www.dfc.gov/media/press-releases/dfc-announces-investments-supporting-development-along-lobito-corridor)). USAspending records it as award 9000116961 to "PENSANA RARE EARTH TA", signed 24 September 2024, for a "feasibility study for mid-stream processing of rare earth minerals in Angola". It shows **US$3.4m obligated, US$1,954,085 paid out**, one transaction with no modifications, and a **performance period ending 24 September 2026** ([USAspending](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/ASST_NON_9000116961_077)). It is the only federal award of any kind to a Pensana-named recipient between FY2008 and FY2026. Searches for "Ozango" and "Longonjo" found nothing relevant ([USAspending API](https://api.usaspending.gov/api/v2/search/spending_by_award/)). Pensana's FY2025 annual report says about US$0.5m had been drawn by June 2025, so most of the payments came in FY2026 ([AR25](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Pensana-integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf)).

In 2026 DFC also published "investment story" pages. They describe "Project Development funding" for Pensana to test material at Longonjo and advance refining, and repeat Pensana's earlier schedule of 20ktpa of mixed rare earth carbonate (MREC) in 2027 ([DFC investment story](https://www.dfc.gov/investment-story/expanding-rare-earth-processing-angola-production-us); [DFC, related page](https://www.dfc.gov/investment-story/supporting-development-rare-earth-processing-facility-angola)). **Inferred:** this is the same US$3.4m grant under DFC's newer label, because USAspending shows no second award. The page is now out of date, since Pensana itself has moved first production to late 2028. **Unknown:** whether the grant was extended past 24 September 2026 or whether the roughly 43% not yet paid out will lapse. No modification has been recorded. No DFC loan, equity, guarantee or political-risk insurance for Pensana or Ozango was found. DFC's Africa deliverable at the August 2026 roundtable went to Harena Rare Earths in Madagascar ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/08/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-announces-billions-in-new-deals-and-investments-to-power-american-mining/)). DFC money mentioned in the VAC/eVAC announcements went to eVAC, not to Pensana.

Other agencies contribute very little. **USGS** Fact Sheet 2024-3029 lists Longonjo descriptively, as being at the financing stage with start-up expected in 2025 ([USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/fs20243029/full)). The **State Department's** 2026 Angola Investment Climate Statement reportedly says FSDEA "has invested significantly in the Pensana rare earths mine project". That wording comes from a search snippet and is **reported, not confirmed**, because state.gov returned HTTP 403 ([State ICS 2026](https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/angola)). The US Embassy in Luanda has published nothing naming Pensana or Longonjo ([embassy search](https://ao.usembassy.gov/?s=Pensana)). No award or announcement was found from the Department of War/DoD, DOE, USTDA or Commerce. **Congress:** site searches of congress.gov, house.gov and senate.gov found no hearing, letter or bill naming Pensana or Longonjo, but congress.gov's full-text search, including the Congressional Record, was blocked (403). That check therefore remains **unknown** and needs to be done by hand.

## The August roundtable gave Pensana a seat, not money

The earlier research gave the date of the White House event as 10–11 August, and this report corrects it. **Confirmed:** the event was President Trump's "Roundtable on American Mining", held at the State Department on **Friday 7 August 2026**. The White House fact sheet announced "over $2 billion" in projects plus more than $180m for mining schools; the press reported it as US$3bn and said 200+ executives attended. The named recipients were Sila Nanotechnologies (US$1.4bn), Sunrise Energy Metals (US$400m), Niron Magnetics (US$150m) and Strategic Bauxite through the Department of War; 5E Advanced Materials, Westwater and Global Advanced Materials (US$58m combined) through EXIM; and Harena through DFC ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/08/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-announces-billions-in-new-deals-and-investments-to-power-american-mining/); [EXIM release](https://www.exim.gov/news/trump-administration-announces-58m-major-new-critical-mineral-deals-white-house-roundtable)). **Pensana, Angola and Longonjo appear nowhere** in the fact sheet, the EXIM release or Secretary Rubio's remarks ([State, Rubio remarks](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/08/secretary-of-state-marco-rubio-at-president-trumps-roundtable-on-american-mining/)). A whitehouse.gov search for "Pensana" returns no results ([WH search](https://www.whitehouse.gov/?s=Pensana)).

Pensana's RNS of Monday 10 August was headlined "Trump announces US$3 billion support for U.S. critical minerals supply chains". It said CEO Tim George was "one of the few international mining executives invited to a private briefing with the President" and that he afterwards spoke with David Copley of the Executive Office of the President and the National Security Council ([Pensana RNS 10 Aug 2026](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/prn/pensana--pre/trump-announces-us-3-billion-support-for-u-s-/9712159)). His attendance and the Copley conversation are **reported**, by Pensana only, because no attendee list has been published. **Inferred:** with 200+ attendees and a government-labelled "roundtable", "private briefing" describes the event generously. The headline borrows a government figure from which Pensana received nothing. The PDF metadata of the RNS names "Andre Pienaar" as author ([RNS PDF](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Trump-announces-US3-billion-support-for-U.S.-critical-minerals-supply-chains.pdf)). Who this person is, and what role they had, is **unknown**. A person of that name founded the investor C5 Capital, but the match is unverified and should be treated only as a lead.

**FARA and lobbying.** The full FARA foreign-principals file (17,770 rows) has **no entry for Pensana, Ozango, Cascade Natural Resources or Sable Min**, and no current entry for FSDEA ([FARA bulk data](https://efile.fara.gov/bulk/zip/FARA_All_ForeignPrincipals.csv.zip)). UNITA registered an agent in June 2026, and the sovereign fund last had a FARA agent (Dutko Grayling) in 2013. **Unknown:** whether Angola's agents work on the Lobito Corridor or minerals, because their supplemental statements were not read. **Unknown:** domestic lobbying under the LDA. lda.senate.gov now redirects to lda.gov, whose API returned HTTP 503 twice, so Pensana's LDA status has not been checked ([lda.gov API](https://lda.gov/api/v1/filings/?client_name=pensana)).

## US companies treat Pensana as one MOU among many

**Confirmed.** An EDGAR full-text search for "Pensana" from January 2024 to 19 October 2026 returns 176 hits. Nearly all are fund holdings reports from Sprott, SPDR, DFA and Empower, which show that US ETFs own PRE shares and nothing more ([EDGAR FTS](https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=%22Pensana%22&dateRange=custom&startdt=2024-01-01&enddt=2026-10-19)). Only one operating-company filing in that window names Pensana: Energy Fuels' 8-K exhibit of 14 July 2026. It says VAC has "Numerous MOUs in place with upstream rare earth companies including Torngat Metals, Aclara Resources, Cyclic Materials, and Pensana" ([Energy Fuels 8-K Ex 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1385849/000106299326003630/exhibit99-1.htm)). Pensana has **no SEC company record**: no CIK, F-1, 20-F or Form D ([EDGAR company search](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?company=pensana&type=&dateb=&owner=include&count=40&action=getcompany)). **Inferred:** after more than a year of Nasdaq "preparations", there is no public filing, so a US listing is not imminent. A confidential draft registration could exist and is **unknown**.

**VAC/eVAC and Energy Fuels.** **Confirmed** by the press: VAC and Pensana signed a non-binding MOU on 22 October 2025. It covers an initial five-year MREC offtake for eVAC's magnet plant in Sumter, South Carolina, which is planned to make 2,000 tpa of magnets at first and 12,000 tpa by 2029, with pricing still to be agreed ([Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/pensana-german-company-seek-to-establish-mine-to-magnet-supply-chain-in-the-us-2025-10-22)). Energy Fuels agreed on 23 June 2026 to buy VAC for about US$1.9bn in enterprise value. Closing is expected in early 2027, subject to approvals ([Adamas Intelligence](https://www.adamasintel.com/energy-fuels-agrees-1-9b-vac-acquisition-pentagon-commits-725m-to-u-s-separation-facility/)). Energy Fuels' own feedstock comes from monazite (Chemours in the US, Donald in Australia, Vara Mada in Madagascar and Bahia in Brazil), which it processes at White Mesa, Utah. It is also acquiring ASM, which its deck says "is able to supply raw materials for VAC's hard magnet products" ([Energy Fuels 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1385849/000106299326003630/exhibit99-1.htm); [Energy Fuels 10-Q Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001385849/000138584926000029/efr-20260630.htm)). Pensana does not appear in the 10-Q. **Inferred:** after the deal closes, eVAC will have an in-house mine-to-magnet chain, which makes Angolan MREC that still needs separation a lower priority. The MOU has not been cancelled, but the strategy of the company that will own the counterparty has moved towards its own supply. That matters because the VAC MOU is the offtake Pensana uses to qualify for SCRI.

**ReElement / American Resources.** **Confirmed** as a press release: on 23 June 2025 American Resources (NASDAQ: AREC) announced an MOU between ReElement and Pensana. It "outlines a framework" for a *proposed* offtake of up to 20,000 tpa of MREC over five years, "pricing to be agreed", linked to ReElement's refineries in Indiana ([ACCESS Newswire via Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/85756/reelement-technologies-corporation-and-pensana-plc-join-forces-to-advance-one-of-largest-global-rare-earth-oxide-supply-chain-partnerships)). **Confirmed absence:** AREC's SEC filings never mention Pensana ([EDGAR FTS, AREC CIK](https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=%22Pensana%22&ciks=0001590715)). **Reported:** AREC's 2025 10-K booked a gain on deconsolidating ReElement, which means ReElement is no longer a consolidated subsidiary. This comes from a search summary because the primary release returned 403 ([ACCESS Newswire](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/metals-and-mining/american-resources-corporation-advances-rare-earth-and-critical-mineral-platform-1168485)). **Inferred:** on the US side the MOU looks dormant, though no one has terminated it. Pensana's FY2025 report also lists Hanwa and Toyota Tsusho each proposing up to 100% of Phase 1 output, so the MOUs together exceed what Longonjo is planned to produce ([AR25](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Pensana-integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf)). **Confirmed absence:** Pensana has named no US site, state or subsidiary. The physical US links in its "US mine-to-magnet" plan, in Indiana and South Carolina, belong to its counterparties.

## Washington's Africa push favours Lobito, but spreads its bets

The White & Case article "Not just the prize: How Africa is shaping the China-US race for critical minerals" **could not be read in full**. whitecase.com returned HTTP 403, the Wayback Machine has no snapshot, and no republication was found. Its authors and date are **unknown**. Search snippets give its core argument: "Africa is not simply the prize in this contest", and African governments are "increasingly shaping the terms of access to their resources, using regulatory power, state participation and industrial policy". The snippets contrast China's two decades of "combining mine ownership with financing, processing capacity and infrastructure" with a US approach based on "commercial partnerships, development finance and diplomacy". They cite the Lobito Corridor and the December 2025 US–DRC Strategic Partnership Agreement, which reportedly gives US firms a right of first offer on designated deposits ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-our-thinking/not-just-prize-how-africa-shaping-china-us-race-critical-minerals)). No snippet shows it mentioning Pensana, Longonjo or Angolan rare earths, so it should not be cited as discussing them. It is also not the same piece as the October 2025 article in The Conversation, which has a similar theme ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202510130017.html)).

The thesis fits Longonjo. Angola shapes the terms of access as a co-owner: FSDEA holds about 26.7% of Ozango by the Angolan count, against Pensana's own figure of 68.4% for itself ([Angolan Mining Magazine](https://angolanminingmag.ao/novidade-novos-elementos-de-terras-raras-descobertos-no-longonjo/)). In 2025–26 US policy turned transactional. It added a Defense Production Act invocation in June 2026 ([CIPS](https://www.cips-cepi.ca/2026/06/18/trump-africa-critical-minerals-and-the-global-order/)), minerals-for-security deals with the DRC and Rwanda, MOUs with Guinea and Morocco at the February 2026 ministerial (Angola is not reported as a signatory), and the US$10bn EXIM-backed "Project Vault" reserve ([Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/02/scrutiny-grows-over-drc-us-minerals-deal-even-as-other-african-nations-sign-up/)). The Lobito Corridor survived the change of administration. DFC's US$553m railway loan closed in July 2026 ([DFC](https://dfc.gov/media/press-releases/dfc-ceo-ben-black-signs-loan-agreement-lobito-atlantic-railway-securing)), and the DRC and Angola signed a new Lobito deal on 28 August 2026 ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/08/28/2026/dr-congo-and-angola-sign-new-lobito-deal)). In October 2026 DFC's Africa head said it was "considering more investments in rare earth developments" and moving towards equity, without naming any projects ([Daily Caller, citing Reuters](https://dailycaller.com/2026/10/12/us-dfc-africa-investments-critical-mineral-race-china/)).

That spread of bets is the important point for Pensana. DFC has backed several competing African rare earth projects: Songwe Hill in Malawi (US$4.6m) ([Energy Capital & Power](https://energycapitalpower.com/malawis-songwe-rare-earth-project-secures-4-6m-dfc-funding/)), Phalaborwa in South Africa through TechMet ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/u-s-invests-in-project-to-remove-rare-earth-minerals-despite-differences-with-south-africa)) and Harena in Madagascar. The large US commitments have gone to domestic or Americas assets, such as the DoD equity in MP Materials and DFC's US$565m for Serra Verde in Brazil ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)). China suspended its October 2025 controls, and the suspension now runs to 10 January 2027; its April 2025 heavy rare earth licensing is still in force ([RAWMATERIALS.net](https://rawmaterials.net/rare-earth-export-controls-china-confirms-extension-of-trade-arrangements-with-u-s/)). That keeps a premium on non-Chinese supply without forcing an emergency. **Inferred:** Longonjo's in-country MREC, its Lobito rail link and its heavy rare earth potential make it relevant to Washington, but it is not indispensable. It sits in a crowded EXIM pipeline and was left out of the August package. Its US-government status is not that of a flagship.

Neither is a financing commitment, and the large instrument (EXIM's guarantee) depends on conditions Pensana has not met: a posted ESIA, signed US offtake and, according to the interim accounts, Cascade's US$150m arriving to satisfy the Absa equity conditions ([H1-26](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Pensana-Interim-results-for-the-six-months-ended-31-December-2025.pdf)). The fairest counter-argument is that EXIM rarely lists a project it has no intention of pursuing, and that DFC chose to profile Longonjo in 2026. Both show genuine interest from the agencies. But interest is not commitment, and Pensana's present-tense wording ("Guaranteed", "backing") reads as if the commitment already exists.

## Conclusion

The US record narrows the Pensana investment question rather than settling it. Washington's commitment to Longonjo is currently a filing number and a mostly spent study grant. That is consistent with a project the agencies are watching, not one they have chosen to back. The decisive variables are not in Washington. They are a signed US offtake, which Energy Fuels' takeover of VAC may make harder to obtain, and Cascade's US$150m, which the Absa/EXIM debt waits on. Washington's support is therefore conditional on the same unresolved financing that already hangs over the company, and it cannot rescue that financing.

The practical takeaway is to read Pensana's US claims against the EXIM and Federal Register pages, which change visibly when anything real happens. An ESIA posting would be the first hard evidence that the "guarantee" is on its way to becoming one. While that page still says "Not yet publicly available", the accurate description of US support is "application pending", not "U.S. EXIM Guaranteed".
