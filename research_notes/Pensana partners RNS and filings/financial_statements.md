At 31 Dec 2025 (unaudited), Pensana had **cash of $19.6m**, **total assets of $117.6m** and **net assets of $96.9m**. ## 2. Going concern: material uncertainty and assumptions

### Takeaway
**Every recent set of accounts carries a material uncertainty over going concern.** BDO's FY2025 opinion is **unmodified, with a "Material uncertainty related to going concern" section**, and going concern was also a key audit matter in FY2024. The H1-26 interim repeats that conditions "indicate a material uncertainty". The basis rests on: the main Longonjo financing meeting its conditions precedent and being deployed; FSDEA converting or restructuring its $15m bridge; new funding to pay overdue UK (Saltend-related) contractors; and those contractors continuing to support the company.

### Cited Findings
- **AR25 directors' statement:** the directors prepared a cash flow forecast to **31 Dec 2026**. "The forecast indicates that immediate funding is required to settle existing overdue project-related contractor balances in the UK and to also provide working capital to the group." Going concern "is dependent on meeting the conditions precedent to secure the deployment of the main Longonjo Project financing, conversion of the US$15 million FSDEA loan facility, securing additional funding to settle existing overdue project-related contractor balances in the UK … along with continued contractors support … These circumstances indicate the existence of material uncertainties which may cast significant doubt…" ([AR25 pp.11–12](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Pensana-integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf))
- **AR25 auditor (BDO LLP):** "MATERIAL UNCERTAINTY RELATED TO GOING CONCERN … indicate that material uncertainties exist that may cast significant doubt on the Group's and the Parent Company's ability to continue as going concerns … Our opinion is not modified in respect of this matter." Going concern was a KAM in both 2025 and 2024. BDO's procedures included "Reviewing external legal confirmations … confirming that no legal proceedings had been initiated against the Group in relation to the historical unsettled creditor balances" and inspecting term sheets with FSDEA, AFC and ABSA ([AR25 pp.88–89](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Pensana-integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf)).
- **H1-26 interim (31 Dec 2025):** the forecast runs to **30 Jun 2027**. "The uncertainty around going concern is limited to the company's ability to settle the remaining balance outstanding under the FSDEA bridging loan (US$8.7 million) as well as the deployment of the remaining funds towards the construction of the Longonjo Rare Earth Mine." Going concern "is dependent on securing such additional funding given the forecast expenditure". "Although conditions … indicate a material uncertainty … the funding approvals received have provided comfort to the Board." Contemplated financing: "further issues of equity, export credit-backed debt financing and/or issuing a green bond" ([H1-26 pp.4–5, 17–18](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Pensana-Interim-results-for-the-six-months-ended-31-December-2025.pdf)).
- H1-26 states that the FY25 statutory auditor's report "was unqualified but drew attention to a material uncertainty in relation to going concern" ([H1-26 p.17](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Pensana-Interim-results-for-the-six-months-ended-31-December-2025.pdf)).
- The H1-26 risk section adds: "Additional funding will be required to settle existing project-related contractor balances in the UK." It also says the group would need to "refinance or restructure the FSDEA facility" if the main financing is not achieved ([H1-26 p.9](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Pensana-Interim-results-for-the-six-months-ended-31-December-2025.pdf)).
- Events after H1 that bear on the going concern assumptions:
  - Cascade paid only $15m of $165m; $150m was still "outstanding" at 6 Aug 2026 ([RNS 6 Aug 2026](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Update-on-Cascade-Financing-6-August-2026.pdf)).
  - On 25 Sep 2026: "outstanding detailed construction design activities, high-cost site activities and long term procurement items have been ramped down until the financing has been finalised … the overall construction schedule is expected to be extended from the previously announced 2028 to late 2028" ([RNS 25 Sep 2026](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Update-on-Longonjo-Financing-25th-September-2026.pdf)).

### Inferences
- The going-concern premise of "funding approvals" from March 2025 (FSDEA/AFC/Absa, $268m) appears to have been replaced in 2026 by Cascade ($165m) plus Absa ($160m, US EXIM-backed). Neither had closed by Sep 2026. The FY2026 accounts are therefore very likely to carry a material uncertainty again, and possibly a sharper one. **This is unverified.**
- The 25 Sep 2026 RNS calls 2028 the "previously announced" date, but every earlier 2026 RNS (17 Apr, 8 Jun) and H1-26 said first production or commissioning was in **2027**. The company's own wording is inconsistent, and it represents a slip of at least one year.

### Gaps
- The FY2026 going-concern note and BDO's FY2026 opinion are not yet public.

## 4. Debt and financing structure

### Takeaway
On balance sheet, the debt is small and all short term. It consists of the **FSDEA (ASF Yova) bridging loan**: $15m drawn, $7.5m converted at 24p in Dec 2025, $8.66m left, secured on Pensana's Ozango shares and charged at SONIA + 2%. The second element is an **external facility from a "UK-based investment house"** with lender warrants: 12% interest, 12-month drawdowns, $2.6m at 31 Dec 2025, secured by share charges over Sable Min, SBLRTHS and Saltend Magnet Metals. Companies House shows an all-assets charge to **RiverFort** that was satisfied in March 2026. The project debt is not yet signed: the March 2025 package was $268m (FSDEA, AFC, Absa), and by 2026 it had become **Absa $160m (US EXIM-backed, proposed)** plus **Cascade $165m equity**, of which $15m has been received.

### Inferences
- An indicative funding gap (my calculation from company figures): $250m capex less $36m spent (Jun 2026) leaves about $214m of capex outstanding. Financing costs, the HREE circuit, corporate overhead to first production and the remaining $8.66m FSDEA bridge come on top. The identified sources ($150m Cascade plus $160m Absa/EXIM) would cover this if both closed. **Neither is committed.** Without them, the shortfall is effectively the whole remaining capex, about $210m+.
- The headline capex rose from $217m (2024–25) to $250m (June 2026). The company has not reconciled the two figures. The increase may reflect scope changes or cost growth, or it may be a different basis that includes some financing costs. **This is unverified.**
