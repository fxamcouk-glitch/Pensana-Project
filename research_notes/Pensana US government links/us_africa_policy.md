## Q2. How does US policy treat African critical minerals in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
Washington has moved from aid to transactional deals and finance:
- executive orders and the Defense Production Act,
- minerals-for-security deals (DRC, Rwanda) and memoranda of understanding signed at the February 2026 ministerial (Guinea, Morocco),
- the $10 billion EXIM "Project Vault" stockpile,
- a DFC that is shifting towards equity.

The Lobito Corridor survived the change of administration and has been expanded. Its $553 million DFC railway loan reached financial close in July 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Executive actions:**
  - An executive order on 20 March 2025 aimed at urgently increasing domestic mineral production.
  - On 11 June 2026 Trump invoked the Defense Production Act to speed up production, after reports that the defence stockpile was low.
  — [CIPS, 18 June 2026](https://www.cips-cepi.ca/2026/06/18/trump-africa-critical-minerals-and-the-global-order/)
- **$3 billion package, 7 August 2026:** announced at a State Department roundtable. It was mostly Pentagon Office of Strategic Capital money:
  - $1.4 billion to Sila Nanotechnologies,
  - $400 million to Sunrise Energy Metals,
  - $150 million to Niron Magnetics,
  - about $58 million from EXIM to Westwater, Global Advanced Metals and 5E.
  
  None of it went to an African project. — [TXF News](https://www.txfnews.com/news/74638/Trump-touts-3bn-in-minerals-projects-to-counter-China); [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/trump-unveils-3b-to-boost-mining/)
- **US–DRC Strategic Partnership Agreement (December 2025):** signed alongside the US-mediated DRC–Rwanda peace deal. It gives the US greater access to DRC minerals. Rwanda signed its own partnership in December. Congolese civil society ("Le Congo n'est pas à vendre") calls it "a race for minerals at any cost", and lawyers challenged it in the Constitutional Court in January 2026. — [Mongabay, February 2026](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/02/scrutiny-grows-over-drc-us-minerals-deal-even-as-other-african-nations-sign-up/); [State Department](https://www.state.gov/strategic-partnership-agreement-between-the-government-of-the-united-states-of-america-and-the-government-of-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo)
- **February 2026 Critical Minerals Ministerial in Washington:** Guinea signed a memorandum of understanding on 5 February 2026, and Morocco signed one at the same ministerial. Angola is **not** reported as a signatory. — [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/02/scrutiny-grows-over-drc-us-minerals-deal-even-as-other-african-nations-sign-up/)
- **Project Vault:** a US strategic critical-minerals reserve backed by an initial $10 billion from EXIM. — [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2026/02/scrutiny-grows-over-drc-us-minerals-deal-even-as-other-african-nations-sign-up/); also cited by [CSIS, April 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- **DFC in Africa (October 2026):**
  - It plans to expand direct equity investments, including in African critical-minerals projects.
  - It has more than $14 billion committed in Africa, over $3 billion of it in critical minerals.
  - It is "considering more investments in rare earth developments" but has not named them.
  - Loans, guarantees and political-risk insurance remain its main tools.
  - Source: Vibhuti Jain, DFC Regional MD for Africa, via Reuters.
  — [Daily Caller, 12 October 2026](https://dailycaller.com/2026/10/12/us-dfc-africa-investments-critical-mineral-race-china/)
- **Lobito Corridor:**
  - The DFC's $553 million loan to Lobito Atlantic Railway reached financial close in July 2026.
  - EXIM approved $900 million, then a further $1.6 billion, for solar and water infrastructure in southern Angola.
  - A State Department official called the corridor part of the effort "to secure supply chains for essential minerals and ensure purchases for the US".
  — [search summary of angolaexpert / Rio Times / State Department sources](https://www.angolaexpert.com/en/angola-partners-united-states-of-america/); DFC loan signing: [DFC press release](https://dfc.gov/media/press-releases/dfc-ceo-ben-black-signs-loan-agreement-lobito-atlantic-railway-securing)
- The DRC and Angola signed a new Lobito deal on 28 August 2026. — [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/08/28/2026/dr-congo-and-angola-sign-new-lobito-deal)
- In February 2026 the US described "very important partnerships in Angola, such as the Lobito Corridor". — [Ver Angola](https://www.verangola.net/va/en/022026/Politics/47890/The-US-highlights-%E2%80%9Cvery-important-partnerships-in-Angola-such-as-the-Lobito-Corridor%E2%80%9D.htm)
- **US–Angola relations:**
  - A 2026–2031 defence cooperation roadmap was agreed in Luanda on 5–6 August 2026.
  - A 12.5% US tariff applies to Angolan goods.
  - Two-way trade is small: Angola exported US$351.8 million to the US and imported US$410.2 million in 2024.
  - Lourenço is hedging: Sonangol sought a US$4.8 billion Chinese loan for the Lobito refinery.
  
  These come from a search-summary synthesis of several sources; the primary reference is the [State Department 2026 Investment Climate Statement: Angola](https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/angola). See also [ECFR, "Caught in the Corridor"](https://ecfr.eu/article/caught-in-the-corridor-angola-europe-and-america/), which says control of critical-mineral supply chains now dominates US thinking on Angola (analysis/opinion).
- Angola's geology plan says the country has 36 of the 51 key critical minerals, including rare earths, most of them untapped. — [Geopolitical Monitor](https://www.geopoliticalmonitor.com/angola-bets-on-critical-minerals-and-the-lobito-corridor/)

### Inferences
- The US toolkit for Africa now pairs security or peace with mineral access. The DRC is the template. Angola's relationship with the US runs through the Lobito Corridor, defence cooperation and EXIM energy lending, not a minerals-for-security deal.

### Gaps
- I found no source for the "Ukraine deal model" being applied in Africa. I did not research the April 2025 Section 232 critical-minerals order, nor whether DFC reauthorisation happened in 2025–2026.
- I could not confirm any formal "2026 Africa critical-minerals initiative" by name, beyond the February 2026 ministerial, the DFC equity push and Project Vault.
- I could not confirm any 2026 US–Angola minerals memorandum of understanding.

## Q3. Where do Angolan rare earths and Longonjo appear in US or Western strategies?

### Takeaway
Longonjo is one of the few African rare-earth projects with **named US government support**:
- a DFC grant of US$3.4 million,
- an EXIM letter of interest (LOI) for US$160 million under the Supply Chain Resiliency Initiative (SCRI), cited by CSIS,
- the CEO's attendance at the August 2026 presidential minerals briefing.

But the support is **non-binding or small**, and Longonjo was not among the August 2026 $3 billion awards. It is not recognised in policy as a flagship asset at the level of MP Materials or Serra Verde.

### Cited Findings
- **CSIS, 27 April 2026** (Gracelin Baskaran and Meredith Schwartz) lists EXIM's rare-earth LOIs:
  - "$160 million to Pensana's Longonjo project in Angola",
  - $200 million to REalloys (Canada),
  - $120 million to Tanbreez (Greenland).
  
  EXIM's rare-earth LOIs total nearly $4 billion, so letters of interest are widespread. — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- **EXIM LOI (28 October 2025):**
  - up to 100% of the US$160 million Longonjo debt,
  - conditional on selling products directly into the US under the SCRI,
  - linked to a memorandum of understanding with Vacuumschmelze (VAC) for offtake to eVAC's magnet plant in Sumter, South Carolina.
  
  At that time the mine was described as costing US$217 million, with production from early 2027. — [Pensana RNS (PDF)](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/U.S.-EXIM-debt-funding-to-accelerate-U.S.-mine-to-magnet-supply-chain-.pdf)
- **DFC:** describes Longonjo as believed to be one of the largest developing rare-earth deposits in the world and cites its proximity to the Lobito railway. It gave a US$3.4 million grant for feasibility work on expanding Longonjo and on the Coola project. — [DFC investment story](https://www.dfc.gov/investment-story/supporting-development-rare-earth-processing-facility-angola); [TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/pensana-secures-us-grant-for-mine-expansion-studies)
- **Diplomatic signal, April 2025:** US Ambassador James Story, 15 ambassadors from G7 countries, PGII (the G7 Partnership for Global Infrastructure and Investment), the EU and the UN attended Longonjo's groundbreaking during a Lobito Corridor tour. — [Rare Earth Exchanges, 8 April 2025](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/lobito-corridor-2/)
- **August 2026 White House briefing:**
  - Pensana CEO Tim George was invited to a private briefing with President Trump and Secretary of State Rubio.
  - He then talked with David Copley of the Executive Office of the President and National Security Council.
  - The release puts Longonjo at **US$250 million, about 25% complete, with first MREC (mixed rare earth carbonate) in 2028**, and plans a heavy rare-earth circuit producing more than 122 tonnes a year of dysprosium and terbium.
  
  Pensana received no funding from the $3 billion package. — [Pensana RNS, 10 August 2026 (PDF)](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Trump-announces-US3-billion-support-for-U.S.-critical-minerals-supply-chains.pdf)
- **Schedule and cost drift (factual comparison):**
  - October 2025: US$217 million, first output early 2027 ([RNS](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/U.S.-EXIM-debt-funding-to-accelerate-U.S.-mine-to-magnet-supply-chain-.pdf)).
  - March 2026: "Construction on track for 2027 first production" ([Pensana project page](https://pensana.co.uk/longonjo-rare-earths-refinery-project/)).
  - August 2026: US$250 million, 2028 ([RNS](https://pensana.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Trump-announces-US3-billion-support-for-U.S.-critical-minerals-supply-chains.pdf)).
- Logistics: output travels about 273 km to the Lobito port along the corridor that the US and EU have helped finance. — [Pensana project page](https://pensana.co.uk/longonjo-rare-earths-refinery-project/) / search summary
- Ownership: Pensana 84%, Angola's sovereign wealth fund 10%, others 6% (project page). This will be diluted at Ozango (Pensana's Angolan subsidiary that is developing Longonjo) to about 68.4% after the US$25 million financing. — [Pensana](https://pensana.co.uk/longonjo-rare-earths-refinery-project/); [Bolsamania RNS repost, 4 February 2026](https://www.bolsamania.com/nota-de-prensa/mercados/pensana-plc---update-on-us25-million-longonjo-mine-finance--21605840.html)

### Inferences
- In Washington, Longonjo counts as a credible non-Chinese source of light rare earths, plus potentially heavy rare earths. Its US status rests on the VAC/eVAC South Carolina offtake, the condition for SCRI eligibility, more than on Angola itself.
- It sits in a crowded EXIM LOI pipeline of about $4 billion.

### Gaps
- I found no Longonjo or Angolan rare-earth mentions by the Atlantic Council, Stimson, USIP, the IEA or EU Global Gateway in the searches I ran. These were not searched exhaustively. Angola's rare earths are referenced in general terms only.

## Q4. China's position and competing projects

### Takeaway
China's controls are suspended but not removed:
- April 2025: heavy rare-earth licensing, which stays in force.
- October 2025: wider rules including a foreign direct product rule.
- Late October 2025: a one-year suspension, now extended to 10 January 2027.

Longonjo competes for US capital with other African rare-earth projects that also have DFC or US interest, notably Songwe Hill, Phalaborwa, Kangankunde and Ampasindava.

### Cited Findings
- Timeline of China's controls (CSIS):
  - April 2025: restrictions on heavy rare earths and magnets.
  - October 2025: a stricter regime including a foreign direct product rule and limits on technology and staff leaving China.
  - Late October 2025: the Trump–Xi agreement to suspend the October rules for one year.
  - November 2025: easing, with uneven flows to the US.
  — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- After the September 2026 Trump–Xi summit in Washington, China's commerce ministry extended the trade arrangements, including the suspension, to **10 January 2027**. The April 2025 heavy rare-earth licensing was never suspended. — [RAWMATERIALS.net](https://rawmaterials.net/rare-earth-export-controls-china-confirms-extension-of-trade-arrangements-with-u-s/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/commodities/articles/china-rare-earth-export-pause-123614106.html)
- Per the IEA, China controls more than 90% of refining for rare earths and graphite. — cited in [Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/10/12/us-dfc-africa-investments-critical-mineral-race-china/)
- US financing benchmarks:
  - the Department of Defense (DoD) took a $400 million equity stake in MP Materials, with a price floor of $110/kg NdPr (neodymium-praseodymium) for 10 years,
  - DoD's Office of Strategic Capital lent $150 million to MP Materials,
  - DoD committed $620 million to Vulcan Elements,
  - the DFC pledged $565 million to Serra Verde (Brazil),
  - the US Trade and Development Agency gave Altona Rare Earths (Mozambique) a $1.875 million grant.
  — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- African competitors:
  - **Mkango, Songwe Hill (Malawi):** $4.6 million from the DFC in September 2025, and a reported $100 million DFC loan under consideration. — [Energy Capital & Power](https://energycapitalpower.com/malawis-songwe-rare-earth-project-secures-4-6m-dfc-funding/)
  - **Lindian, Kangankunde (Malawi):** final investment decision in August 2025 with US$59 million, and an Iluka offtake plus a US$20 million loan. US officials visited, but no US funding has been announced. — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-industry/1812-51499-us-interest-in-malawi-rare-earths-extends-to-kangankunde-project)
  - **Rainbow, Phalaborwa (South Africa):** about $50 million of DFC-linked money through TechMet, released at construction, which is expected in early 2027. — [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/u-s-invests-in-project-to-remove-rare-earth-minerals-despite-differences-with-south-africa)
  - **Ampasindava (Madagascar):** up to $4.84 million from the DFC. — [search summary](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/commodities/articles/china-rare-earth-export-pause-123614106.html) (source attribution uncertain)
- China competes on infrastructure: US$1.4 billion for the TAZARA railway (Tanzania–Zambia), a rival route to Lobito. — [Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/10/12/us-dfc-africa-investments-critical-mineral-race-china/)

### Inferences
- With the suspension now running to January 2027, the urgency premium for non-Chinese supply persists but is not acute. Washington is spreading small grants across several African rare-earth projects rather than backing one.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence of specific Chinese bids for Angolan rare-earth assets or Longonjo.
- I did not verify the status of Ngualla (Tanzania), or 2026 updates for Lynas and MP Materials.

## Q5. What this means for Pensana's ability to secure US EXIM, DFC or allied financing

### Takeaway
Policy tailwinds are real but have not turned into committed US money. The EXIM support is still an LOI or proposed guarantee on a US$160 million ABSA debt package. The main equity, up to US$165 million, comes from **Qatar-backed Cascade**, not Washington, and only US$15 million had been received by August 2026. Rising capex and a slip to 2028 increase the financing need.

### Cited Findings
- Cascade Natural Resources, which is Qatar-backed:
  - announced 4 March 2026,
  - US$15 million for about 3.8% of Pensana plc at 80p a share,
  - US$150 million for 38.2% of Sable Min, the holding company above Ozango and Longonjo.
  
  This was up from an initial US$100 million announced in December 2025. — [Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/cascade-increases-strategic-investment-in-pensana-to-165m-2026-03-04); [Investegate RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/prn/pensana--pre/increased-us-165-million-strategic-investment-/9457728)
- The funds, together with a **proposed US$160 million ABSA debt package guaranteed by US EXIM**, are meant to cover Longonjo, a heavy rare-earth facility, downstream work and a NASDAQ listing. — [TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/pensana-secures-us165m-cascade-investment-to-drive-u-s-mine-to-magnet-strategy)
- At 6 August 2026, US$15 million had been received and the US$150 million balance was "being finalised". — [Kalkine Media](https://kalkinemedia.com/uk/news/announcements/pensana-plc-pre-secures-us15-million-from-qatar-backed-cascade-as-remaining-us150-million-funding-nears-closure)
- The company lists an "Update on Longonjo Financing" dated 25 September 2026. I could not retrieve its content. — [Pensana project page](https://pensana.co.uk/longonjo-rare-earths-refinery-project/)
- **Opinion:** Rare Earth Exchanges says the mine-to-magnet ambitions "still face execution risk". It questions whether the full US$165 million is committed or depends on milestones, and whether the EXIM-backed debt closes on the expected terms. — [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/pensana-secures-first-15m-tranche-but-mine-to-magnet-ambitions-still-face-execution-risk/)
- **Opinion:** The DFC's own Africa lead signals a growing appetite for rare-earth equity in Africa, an opening Pensana could use, though no projects are named. — [Daily Caller citing Reuters](https://dailycaller.com/2026/10/12/us-dfc-africa-investments-critical-mineral-race-china/)
- **Opinion, general:** African mines that still ship ore to China for processing leave US vulnerabilities unchanged. This favours Longonjo's in-country MREC plus the US magnet offtake. — [Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/10/12/us-dfc-africa-investments-critical-mineral-race-china/)
- Pensana is pre-revenue, and its first-half pretax loss widened to US$3.3 million. — [MarketScreener / Alliance News](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/pensana-first-half-loss-widens-remains-upbeat-on-rare-earth-market-ce7e51dbdf8df223)

### Inferences
- Strengths for an EXIM or DFC case:
  - the SCRI fit through the VAC South Carolina offtake,
  - the Lobito link that the US itself financed,
  - an existing DFC relationship,
  - CSIS and White House visibility,
  - heavy rare-earth potential (dysprosium and terbium).
- Constraints:
  - EXIM LOIs are non-binding and widespread (about $4 billion),
  - the August 2026 $3 billion package skipped Longonjo,
  - the DFC is spreading support across rival African projects,
  - the change in timeline and capex (US$217 million to US$250 million, 2027 to 2028) raises completion risk,
  - lenders will probably want the Cascade US$150 million closed before the EXIM-guaranteed ABSA debt reaches financial close.
- Overall: strategically **relevant but not indispensable** to Washington. Pensana is likely to need a blend of US and allied finance. Possible sources include Gulf equity (Cascade), Japanese or European offtakers, and EU Global Gateway or PGII under the Lobito umbrella. This is an inference, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No confirmed EXIM board approval, DFC loan or DFC equity for Longonjo as of October 2026.
- The content of the 25 September 2026 financing update is unknown.
- I found no sell-side analyst notes on the probability of US financing.
